HC-MoE: Summary of Work and Paper Draft

What the study has established through v19, the experiments behind each claim, an honest assessment, and a draft abstract
October 20, 2026 · through report v19
In one sentence. In Qwen3, anomalous routing in the last MoE layer marks the tokens where a larger model helps most; the signal survives every control we ran, generalises beyond the studied benchmarks except on code, and fades to zero as the compute gap between the models closes.

1. Setup

The question is whether a large MoE model’s routing tells us, token by token, where it beats a smaller model of the same family. For each token we treat the per-expert routing scores of the large model’s last MoE layer as p-values against an estimated null, and summarize them with Higher Criticism (HC), a statistic built to detect a few strong departures among many tests. We relate HC to token difficulty (CEbig, CEsmall) and to Intelligence Gain, IG = CEsmall − CEbig.

2. Main findings

Six claims hold up. Grade A = holds under every check run; B = holds, with a named sensitivity or a limited sample.

A robust under every checkB holds, with a named sensitivity
  • A
    1. HC tracks token difficulty in every family.
    31 and 28 of 36 benchmark×family cells are outside the noise floor against CEbig and CEsmall. The sign is family-specific: positive in Qwen, ERNIE and Liquid, negative in Gemma.
  • A
    2. In Qwen3, HC marks the tokens where scale helps.
    Mean r(HC, IG) = +0.058, positive on all nine studied benchmarks, strongest on lambada (+0.131, 95% CI +0.120 to +0.141). With either model’s loss held fixed, within the same token string, the four strongest benchmarks stay positive in every configuration (+0.027 to +0.073). It replicates on held-out halves (r = 0.95 across cells), is not carried by outlier tokens, and is spread over several experts (82% survives removing the strongest).
  • B
    3. The association scales with the compute gap and goes to zero when it closes.
    Holding Qwen3-30B-A3B fixed and growing the dense partner from 0.6B to 8B, the nine-benchmark mean of the scale-specific association falls +0.044 → +0.032 → +0.015 → +0.006.
  • B
    4. HC carries information that simpler last-layer statistics do not.
    After router entropy, top-expert probability, logit gap and hidden-state norm are partialled out, 77% of the scale-specific association remains in competition_math and 49% in lambada (18% in race). The added variance is small, about 0.1% of within-string IG variance.
  • B
    5. The effect generalises out of domain, with code as a code-specific exception.
    On six unseen benchmarks, Qwen meets the pre-registered prediction on news (+0.055), scientific papers (+0.035), chat (+0.014) and dialogue (+0.012), and fails on both code sets. At matched small-model loss, hard prose tokens are where HC marks the most gain and hard code tokens where it marks the least: all 10 code deciles sit below prose (mean gap −0.124).
  • A
    6. Gemma 4 shows a robust reversed association, partly through its shared experts (B).
    r(HC, IG) = −0.045 on the studied grid and negative on all six unseen benchmarks in every configuration. Not explained by the compute gap, format tokens or router confidence; scaling every layer’s shared expert to 0.75 removes 41% of the race inversion at a 3% loss cost.
ERNIE-4.5 and LFM2 show no consistent family-level association with IG: strong cells of both signs cancel across benchmarks.

5. Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Rigorous inference. Noise floors at measured effective sample size, five null estimators, passage bootstrap, held-out replication, exact reproduction checks on every new drop, and pre-registered rules applied as written, including when they went against us.
  • A dose-response, not only a correlation. With the large model fixed, the signal shrinks to zero as the dense partner grows. This is the closest the study comes to causal evidence and the strongest figure.
  • The obvious objections are answered. Both difficulty controls, within-token-string analysis and four same-layer baselines rule out “it is just token difficulty” and “it is just router confidence”.
  • Tested out of domain, with the exception explained. The claim passes on four of six unseen benchmarks chosen in advance, and the code exception is shown to be specific to code rather than to hard tokens.
  • A real family contrast. Gemma’s inversion is robust across 15 benchmarks and partly traced to its shared experts.

Weaknesses

  • Small effects and no application. r ≤ 0.13, and HC adds about 0.1% of within-string IG variance beyond simple router statistics. “So what?” is the most likely rejection reason at a main track.
  • HC is distinct from simpler signals, not better. Top-expert probability predicts IG about as well on its own; the contribution is a diagnostic, not a superior statistic.
  • One layer. Every result comes from the last MoE layer; the layer sweep is deferred.
  • The positive claim rests mostly on one model. Qwen carries it, Gemma inverts, ERNIE and LFM2 are null.
  • Loose ends. race is largely absorbed by the hidden-state norm (18% remains); Gemma’s inversion is only about two-fifths explained; why code reverses is unknown; two drops used a 207k-token subset of fineweb-edu.
  • Observational. Apart from the shared-expert ablation, no experiment intervenes on routing.

6. Draft paper abstract

Routing Anomalies Mark Where Scale Helps: Higher Criticism on Mixture-of-Experts Routing

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language models route each token to a few experts, but whether that routing reveals where model scale pays off is unknown. For each token, we treat the routing scores of a large MoE model’s final MoE layer as p-values against an estimated null and summarize them with Higher Criticism (HC), a statistic designed to detect a few strong departures among many tests. We relate HC to Intelligence Gain (IG), the per-token loss reduction over a dense small model from the same family, using five null estimators, passage-level bootstrap, autocorrelation-corrected effective sample sizes, and decision rules fixed before each follow-up experiment. Across four model pairs (Qwen3, ERNIE-4.5, Gemma 4, LFM2), HC tracks token difficulty in every family. In Qwen3-30B-A3B it also marks where scale helps: the association holds with either model’s loss controlled, within occurrences of the same token string, on held-out text, and on unseen news, scientific and conversational text. Holding the large model fixed, it shrinks steadily to zero as the dense partner grows from 0.6B to 8B parameters, and much of it persists after controlling for router entropy, top-expert probability, logit gap and hidden-state norm. Code is a systematic exception: at matched small-model loss, hard prose tokens are where HC marks the most gain and hard code tokens where it marks the least. Gemma 4 shows a robust reversed association, partly carried by its shared experts. Effects are small (r ≤ 0.13), positioning HC as a diagnostic of scaling benefit rather than a routing signal.

What the paper can leave out

These were exploration or dead ends; omitting them does not change what the claims mean.

  • The retired IG-percentile “layer” slices and the early full-grid significance counts.
  • Top-ventile surge analyses, which depend on the null estimator.
  • Hypotheses tested and rejected along the way: active compute for lambada’s 4B reversal, copying and unpredictable naming for code, the last-layer-only shared-expert arm.
  • The dialogue speaker-tag test (8/10, one configuration short of its rule) and the drops that failed reproduction.

What should stay

Leaving these out would change what the remaining claims mean, and a reviewer can find them.

  • ERNIE and LFM2 in the main results table, one sentence each. Dropping two of four families after seeing them is selective reporting.
  • The code exception. The out-of-domain test was pre-registered on six benchmarks; claiming generalisation without naming code overstates it. Framed as “code-specific” it reads as a finding.
  • “Distinct from, not better than” the simple router signals. Claiming HC is the best predictor would be contradicted by our own baseline numbers.
  • The last-layer scope and the small effect sizes, stated once in the limitations.
